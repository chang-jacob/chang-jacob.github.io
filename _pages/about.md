I am a Ph.D. candidate in Biomedical Data Science at Stanford University, co-advised by Dr. Sylvia Plevritis and Dr. Lu Tian. My research sits at the intersection of statistics, machine learning, and health data science, with a focus on developing methods for robust, interpretable, and reproducible discovery in complex biomedical data.

I am broadly interested in how to identify stable and meaningful signal in the presence of heterogeneity, noise, dependence, and distribution shift. My recent work has centered on spatial and single-cell genomics and histopathology, where I study questions in multi-sample inference, uncertainty-aware modeling, multimodal representation learning, and realistic evaluation. More generally, I aim to build methods that support reliable scientific and clinical insight across a range of biological and health data settings. While my recent work has focused on genomics and histopathology, I am broadly interested in statistical machine learning for diverse biological and health data modalities, including settings closer to clinical decision-making and longitudinal health measurement.

I am originally from Mililani, Hawaiʻi, and earned bachelor's degrees in Statistics and Sociology from the University of Notre Dame. Before graduate school, I worked as a Data Scientist at 84.51°, where I worked on the Health and Wellness Pharmacy Team and the Algorithmic Fairness Team. As a graduate student, I have interned as a computational biologist and bioinformatician at Genentech and Amgen, and in the summer of 2026 I will be a Genomic Data Science intern at Element Biosciences. 

Outside of research, I enjoy reading, playing hockey, and spending time with my wife and our dog.
